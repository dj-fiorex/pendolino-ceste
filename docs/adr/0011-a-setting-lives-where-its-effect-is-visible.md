# A setting lives where its effect is visible

Every mill-wide setting in this app started out on the screen that used it: the Soglia di ritardo on the Lista di recupero, the Etichetta's size and footer on the sheet that prints labels, the two Sms templates on the screen that shows their preview. That worked until the mill's own name and telephone turned out to be read by two screens at once — printed across the foot of an Etichetta and written into every Sms — and there was nowhere to type them that was not somebody else's screen. So there is now a Frantoio screen, and a rule for what may be on it.

The rule is that a setting whose effect you can see while you change it stays on the screen that shows it, and a setting you can only state moves to Il frantoio. The Etichetta's size and the two footer switches redraw the label in front of you; the Sms templates redraw the preview; those stay. The Soglia di ritardo does not: the Lista di recupero is the same list in the same order at one day and at a hundred, and all that changes is which rows are amber — so reading the Soglia back as a sentence loses nothing, and it moves.

The alternative was to move every mill-wide setting and leave the other screens to choose and produce. It would have given one place and one story, at the price of an Admin editing the words of an Sms on one screen while the preview that tells them whether the words are right sits on another. Words go out to two hundred people and cannot be taken back; a label size can be reprinted. That asymmetry is what the rule is protecting.

## Consequences

Il frantoio holds four things — the name, the telephone, the Mittente SMS and the Soglia di ritardo — and the burden of proof is on the fifth. Anything mill-wide that a screen can already show you belongs on that screen; the rule exists to be quoted at a pull request that would move it.

The Lista di recupero keeps the sentence that says what the amber means, now linking an Admin to Il frantoio rather than carrying the form. The Etichette sheet keeps its two footer switches and disables one whose value has not been written yet, pointing at Il frantoio instead of quietly printing nothing — the one place where the split costs a screen something, and it is paid in a sentence.
